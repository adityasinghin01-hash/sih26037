### The gate that is not in the funnel — toolchain access

**This is the single decisive unknown, and it is binary.**

- RoadRunner and Automated Driving Toolbox are **not in MATLAB student licences**. They *are* in
  **Campus-Wide Licences** — so this depends entirely on what KIET's MATLAB licence includes.
  (Sensor Fusion and Navigation Toolbox *are* in student licences.)
- **RoadRunner runs on Windows and Linux only — macOS is not supported.** Verified on the MathWorks
  platform-availability page. Aditya's machine is a Mac, so at least one team member needs a
  Windows/Linux box for the scenario authoring.
- MathWorks has partnered with SIH since 2019, runs webinars per problem statement, and mentors
  teams through the finale. Contact route: hackathon@mathworks.com.

If the licence covers RoadRunner + ADT, feasibility is ~8. If it does not, feasibility drops to ~4
and **the idea is blocked under the "any axis ≤4" rule.** Nothing else about this PS is uncertain.

### Competitor sweep (verified)

- **Swaayatt Robots** (Bhopal, from IIT Roorkee research 2009, road-testing since 2015) — L5 on
  adversarial unstructured traffic; 2024 video shows the vehicle handling pedestrians, dogs, cows,
  tractors and wrong-way scooters. $4M raised in 2024 at a $151M valuation.
- **Minus Zero** (Bengaluru) — camera-first foundation-model autopilot for Indian roads, no HD maps.
- **The real competitive risk is not them — it is the other SIH teams.** MathWorks *ships* most of
  this pipeline as reference examples: `Highway Lane Following`, `Highway Lane Change`,
  `trajectoryOptimalFrenet` (Navigation Toolbox), and a grid-based-tracker urban dynamic-replanning
  example. Every team will assemble the same stack. **Differentiation has to come from the scenario
  design and the planner, not the pipeline.**

### The import move (`hackathon-idea-method` step 4)

Two candidates, both genuinely unaimed at this problem:

1. **Indian mixed traffic is not a lane system — it is a crowd.** Import the **Social Force Model /
   Reciprocal Velocity Obstacles** from pedestrian crowd dynamics. Social force has been applied to
   non-lane-based motorcycle flow (Nguyen & Hanaoka 2011) and to e-bike/car mixed flow, but as a
   *traffic-simulation* model — not as the **ego-vehicle's planner** on Indian roads. Reframing the
   planner as crowd navigation rather than lane-following is a defensible, one-sentence novelty claim.
2. **Conformal prediction on the trajectory predictor** — distribution-free calibrated bounds on
   where each agent can be, so the planner does not merely avoid collisions but *bounds the
   probability* of one. Very active in 2025–26 robotics (e.g. arXiv 2508.05634, crowd navigation;
   arXiv 2212.00278, motion planning among dynamic agents). Gives the demo a number, which is
   exactly what a MathWorks judge rewards.

Pairing them gives one legible sentence: *"we treat Indian traffic as a crowd rather than a road,
and we put a statistical guarantee on the safety margin."*

### Data access — verified

- **IDD (India Driving Dataset, IIIT-H)** — reachable (HTTP 200). Detection set **46,588 images**
  (31,569 train / 10,225 val / 4,794 test); segmentation set **10,003 images**; classes include
  **auto-rickshaw and animal**. Download requires a free account (`/dataset/download/` → 302 to
  login); licence is shown before download. **Account creation not yet tested — do this first.**
- MathWorks Automated Driving Toolbox ships sensor/scenario datasets and RoadRunner sample scenes.

## SIH26047 — Patient Case-Taking Software
**Ministry of Ayush · dept: All India Institute of Ayurveda (AIIA) · Software · MedTech/BioTech/HealthTech**

The PS is far more specific than its title. It names the deliverable "MediKiosk" and specifies four
modules: (A) conversational multimodal voice+touch history engine with adaptive follow-ups
(SOCRATES framework), red-flag triage, and an **AYUSH mode capturing Dashavidha Pariksha**;
(B) handwritten/printed multilingual medical-document OCR with chronological timeline and
abnormal-value flagging; (C) physician-ready structured summary generator; (D) DPDP-Act-2023
consent + **ABHA authentication + FHIR push to HIS**.

### Phase 1 — kill checks

| # | Check | Result |
|:-:|---|---|
| 1 | Real measured problem | ✅✅ **STRONGEST OF EITHER IDEA.** BMJ Open 2017, 67-country study: India's average primary-care consultation is **just over 2 minutes**. Tertiary government hospitals register 4,000–10,000 OPD/day. The ministry cites these itself, so the framing is not ours to defend. |
| 2 | Precise target user base | ✅ PASS. **AIIA New Delhi, ~1,500 OPD patients/day**; 126+ Ayush hospitals already running A-HMIS. Nameable, countable. |
| 3 | Physical / tangible significance | ⚠️ **PARTIAL — the one real flag.** (a) Visible artifact: yes, a kiosk flow and a summary appearing on the doctor's screen. (b) Provable claim: **no.** The headline claim is *consultation time saved*, and proving it needs a real OPD time-motion study we cannot run. **This is the Tenable failure mode returning** — the metric would have to be self-generated. |
| 4 | Judge legibility | ✅ PASS, high. "Doctors get 2 minutes per patient; our kiosk hands them the full history before the patient walks in." |
| 6 | ChatGPT test | ⚠️ **WEAKEST AXIS OF EITHER IDEA.** The core is "an LLM asks adaptive questions and summarises, and OCR extracts fields" — a frontier model does most of it. It survives only on three things: the **ABDM FHIR write** (a real-world action), the **Dashavidha Pariksha → NAMASTE code** ontology (privileged/structurally Indian), and the low-literacy multilingual kiosk interaction. Passes, but not comfortably. |
| 7 | Indian moat | ✅ PASS, strong **if built on NAMASTE + ICD-11 TM2**. 1,941 national Ayush morbidity codes mapped; TM2 released on the WHO ICD-11 browser Feb 2025. No global player has this. |
| 8 | Who pays for the failure | ✅ PASS. Ministry of Ayush, plus NHA's **Digital Health Incentive Scheme pays hospitals per Scan&Share transaction and per EHR generated** — money is already flowing for exactly this behaviour. |
| 9 | Choosing vs losing | ✅ PASS. Doctors are *losing* to throughput, not choosing to skip history. |
| 10 | Government-rail check | ⚠️ **THE GATE — UNVERIFIED.** `sandbox.abdm.gov.in` is live (301 → `/sandbox/v3`, HTTP 200), but `/applications/register` returned **HTTP 503**, and documented signup requires **organisation details plus internal approval (3–4 days)**. Per our standing rule, assume closed until proven open. |
| 11 | No adversarial products | ✅ PASS. |

### Competitor sweep (verified) — this is the problem

| What | Status | What it already does |
|---|---|---|
| **PGI Chandigarh AI kiosk** | **DEPLOYED, Indian govt hospital** | Patient picks Punjabi/Hindi/English, answers spoken questions; system generates a 13-digit ID and the **patient's medical and personal history as a QR code the doctor scans**. This is the PS's core solution, already running. |
| **ZINI** (Delhi, f. 2017) | Funded, operating | Explicitly a **"history-taking bot"**, built on Indian population data, regional languages, plus an OPD management system and medical API. STPI ₹25 lakh NGIS grant 2021; Pre-Series A 2022. |
| **Infermedica Intake** | Mature, global | Pre-visit history/allergy/medication capture, triage, EHR integration; published outcome **20 min → 12.5 min visit time**. |
| **Ada Health** | 60M+ users | Intelligent care navigation at health-system scale. |
| **A-HMIS** (Ayush Grid) | **126+ Ayush hospitals** | Ayush HMIS with **double coding** (Ayush morbidity + ICD-10), **already ABDM-integrated** — creates ABHA, handles registration and follow-up. |
| **ABDM Scan & Share** | **25 crore OPD registrations**, 5,435 facilities, 546 districts (Aug 2026) | QR-based instant OPD registration; wait times ~1 hour → 2–5 min. |

**Read honestly:** registration is built. Voice history-taking in an Indian government hospital is
built. A history-taking bot is built. Intake-plus-EHR is built. Ayush coding is built.
**What is not built as one system:** voice history + handwritten document OCR + Dashavidha Pariksha
+ FHIR push. That slice is real but narrow — and this is exactly the pattern that killed nine ideas
in the original sweep ("the government already built it").

### Data access — verified

- **AI4Bharat / IndicASR** ✅ **open and free.** 22 languages, permissive licences (MIT / Apache 2.0 /
  CC-BY-4.0), production-grade. Hindi WER **~12–18% on clean speech, 22–30% on telephony**.
  A noisy OPD hall sits at the bad end of that range — a live-demo risk.
- **Handwritten prescription OCR** ⚠️ **the technical crux, and genuinely hard.** MIRAGE
  (arXiv 2410.09729) fine-tunes Qwen-VL / LLaVA-1.6 / Idefics2 on **743,118 annotated simulated
  Indian prescription images from 1,133 doctors**; an IEEE DataPort set has 11,340 images.
  Published accuracies span **64% → 98%** depending on method and scope. A hostile judge will hand
  the demo a real crumpled Hindi prescription.
- **NAMASTE / ICD-11 TM2** ✅ 1,941 codes mapped; TM2 live on the WHO ICD-11 browser since Feb 2025.
