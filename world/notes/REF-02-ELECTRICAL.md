# REF-02 · THE ELECTRICAL SYSTEM
Researched 3 Sep 2026 from a state transmission utility specification (OPTCL), the REC
11 kV/415 V overhead line specification, IS:802, CEA clearance rules — and **one shape
measured directly off Aditya's own footage.**

## THE CHAIN, IN ORDER
transmission (132/220/400 kV, lattice towers) → substation → 33 kV → substation →
**11 kV feeder on concrete poles** → **distribution transformer** → **415 V, four wires** →
service drop → meter box on a house wall.
**The lattice towers do NOT feed the village.** They pass overhead going somewhere else.
Build them crossing the road at an angle, ignoring it entirely — that is what real lines do.

## 1 · TRANSMISSION TOWERS
| | 132 kV | 220 kV | 400 kV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structure | lattice, single or double circuit | lattice double circuit | heavy lattice, bundle |
| **Normal span** | **320 m** | **350 m** | ~400 m |
| Ground clearance | **6.10 m** | **7.00 m** | **8.84 m** |
| Right of way | 27 m | 35 m | 46 m |
| Insulator discs | **9** suspension / 10 tension | ~14 | 21–27 |
| Arcing distance | 1530 mm | 2130 mm | 3050 mm |
| Conductor | ACSR **Panther** | ACSR **Zebra** | double **Moose** |
(66 kV RoW 15 m; 765 kV 64–85 m.)

**Tower types by deviation angle** — type-tested as 0–2°, 0–15°, 0–30°, 0–60°:
**A = suspension 0–2° · B = tension 2–15° · C = 15–30° · D = 30–60°.**
Each comes with **+3 m and +6 m body extensions** — that is how a real line follows the ground.
**Height : base width ≈ 5 : 1.** A 33 m tower stands on a 6.6 m square.
Span limits (132 kV): normal max 500 m, min 100 m; broken-wire max 300 m.

**Geometry rules:**
- **X-braces at 45°** — the fabrication standard.
- Legs panelled every **~2 m** to stop buckling.
- Bracing systems by position: **portal** in the bottom panels and extensions, **Warren**
  (double web) through the body and cage, **single web** for narrow towers and cross-arm girders.
- **Earth wire peak set by a 20° shielding angle** from the peak down to the outermost conductor.
- **Insulator disc = 255 mm diameter × 145 mm pitch.** String length = discs × 145 mm.
  9 discs = **1305 mm**. Each disc withstands roughly 10–12 kV — you can read a line's voltage
  off a photograph by counting them.
- **Step bolts: 16 mm dia, 175 mm long, 400 mm apart, on ONE main leg only, from 2.5 m to
  the top.** Plus an **anti-climbing device** (IS:5613) — a flared barbed collar around 3.5 m.
- Accessories that read at distance: hangers, U-bolts, **bird guards**, gusset plates.
- Sag over a 320 m span ≈ 8 m. Always check `tower attach − sag > ground clearance`.

## 2 · THE 11 kV FEEDER — and the shape that is always got wrong
**THE POLE TOP IS A CANDELABRA, NOT A FLAT BAR.** Verified by cropping and enlarging
`video/frames/drive_10_t121.jpg`: a horizontal cross-arm, then **two arms angling up and
outward with a pin insulator on each tip**, and a **third pin insulator on a short centre stalk,
higher than the other two**. The three conductors sit in a **triangle**. This silhouette is on
every pole in every one of Aditya's frames.

| | |
|---|---|
| Pole | **PCC, rectangular, 7.5 / 8.0 / 9.0 m**. Planted **1.5 m** (~1/6 of length) |
| Span | **25–60 m** (use ~45 m) |
| Cross-arm | MS channel **75 × 40 × 6 mm** |
| Our geometry | arm 1.80 m · V tips ±0.60 m raised 0.45 m · centre pin +0.75 m · insulator 0.17 m |
| Phase spacing rule | **not less than 1 % of the span** (45 m span → 450 mm) |
| Pin insulator | S 165 P (IS 2486 Pt-II), 165 mm stalk, creepage 320 mm, 10 kN transverse |
| Strain insulator | tongue-and-clevis, 70 kN, creepage 400 mm |
| Conductor | ACSR 7/2.11 mm (20 mm²), 7/2.59, 7/3.35 |
| Guard-wire arm | extends **≥ 300 mm** beyond the outermost conductor |

**Stays (guy wires):** GI **7/4.0 mm or 7/3.15 mm**, at **40–60° to the pole**, ground
projection **450 mm**, anchor plate **300 × 300 × 6.4 mm**, stay rod 16/19 mm × ≥1800 mm,
stay pit **≥1.8 m from the pole**, strut buried ≥1.2 m.
**Danger plates:** 250 × 200 mm at 11 kV, 200 × 150 mm at 415 V — 1.6 mm steel, white with
signal-red lettering (IS:2551).

## 3 · THE DISTRIBUTION TRANSFORMER — the object on every Indian street
Double-pole structure: **two 8 m PCC poles**, platform between them at ~3.3–3.55 m,
transformer tank above. On top: **three drop-out fuses (12 kV, 100 A, 8 kA)** and
**three lightning arresters (9 kV rms, 5 kA)**. LT distribution box below. Danger plate.

## 4 · 415 V — THE HOUSE SUPPLY
**Four wires: three phases (R, Y, B) and a neutral.** AAC 7/2.21 mm (25 mm²) or 7/3.10 mm
(50 mm²). Lower cross-arm, spans not exceeding 65 m. Service drops sag visibly lower than
everything else and land at a meter box on the wall.

## 5 · THE LAST LAYER — the mess
Cable TV, telephone and illegal taps ride the same poles. Through a town expect **9–14 parallel
runs** at different sags, some spliced, some hanging loose, bundles at two or three poles.
That tangle is the thing that reads as India, and it is free geometry.

## 6 · UNDERGROUND (for completeness)
Trench 35 cm wide; depth ≥75 cm up to 1.1 kV, ≥120 cm above. Brick protection 20×10×10 cm.
Route markers 100 × 100 × 5 mm on angle iron, every 100 m, 0.5 m off the trench.
