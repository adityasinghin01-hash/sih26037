# REF-03 · BUILDINGS, THE TOWN, AND HOW TO BUILD A THOUSAND OF THEM
Researched 3 Sep 2026. Sources: National Building Code of India, IS 6248, building bye-laws,
urban-morphology literature, and a procedural-building technique video read frame by frame.

## 1 · THE METHOD — this is the important part
**Do NOT model 25 whole buildings. Model about 30 small FACADE PARTS.**
A library of parts laid out flat — window, shutter, balcony, AC box, sign board, awning,
drainpipe, grille, staircase, water tank, parapet section, door, meter box, satellite dish,
drying washing, exposed rebar — is scattered across the faces of a **plain box**.
The result looks heavily detailed but is a cube plus shared parts.
**A thousand buildings then cost roughly what thirty parts cost**, because instances share
their geometry.

**Anti-repetition, which is mandatory (bar item 8 — nothing repeats):**
width 2.9–9.5 m · storeys 1/2/3 · a different colour, often per floor · a random clutter mix ·
±2° of rotation. 30 shapes × 4 widths × 3 heights × 10 colours × a clutter draw runs into
thousands of distinct results.

**Detail by distance:** full parts near the camera · shell + roof clutter at mid distance ·
correct silhouette only far away. Past ~400 m the haze has eaten it anyway.

## 2 · DIMENSIONS  (NBC India, IS 6248, bye-laws)
| | |
|---|---|
| Floor to floor incl. slab | **3.0–3.15 m** |
| Minimum clear ceiling | 2.75 m (IS 875 Pt 2) |
| Parapet | minimum **1.0 m**, 1.2 m+ on tall buildings |
| Window sill | **0.9 m** above finished floor |
| Window area | ≥10 % of floor area |
| **Two-storey shop-house** | 6.3 m + 1.0 m parapet = **7.3 m** to parapet top |
| **Three-storey** | ~**10.45 m** |
| Rolling shutter (IS 6248) | **2.4–3.7 m wide, up to 3.0 m high**, laths **75 mm**, steel 0.8–1.2 mm |
| Awning / canopy | max **4.5 m long × 2.4 m projection**, **minimum 2.2 m clear beneath** |
| Cantilever projection | typically up to 0.9 m |
| Beam soffit / slab soffit | min 2.4 m / max 2.7 m |
So a typical roadside shop: **3 m shutter, 3 m tall opening, awning projecting 0.9–2.4 m at
2.2–2.4 m above ground.**

## 3 · ROAD WIDTH GOVERNS BUILDING HEIGHT
Roads under **6 m** restrict buildings to **2 storeys**; 18 m+ roads allow 4+.
Minimum abutting road width for a small residential plot: **4.8–7.2 m**.
This is why a galli has two-storey buildings and the main bazaar has three.

## 4 · TOWN MORPHOLOGY — the layout logic
Indian indigenous towns: **narrow winding streets, irregular plots, mixed land use, NO
building setbacks.** A clear street hierarchy, with **galis lowest** — paved, arcaded, lined
with shops, acting as primary or secondary bazaar streets. Retail runs a mile or two along the
main arteries with subsidiary bazaars branching off. Residential neighbourhoods sit directly
behind the commercial frontage.
**Practical consequences:** the frontage is a continuous wall of building, not detached blocks;
shops face the street with nothing in front of them; the lanes behind are residential and much
quieter than the bazaar 30 m away.

## 5 · THE KUTCHA HOUSE / HUT — the rural counterpart
Frame of wood or bamboo, filled with matting, **cow dung and mud plaster**; roof thatched with
grass, leaves or bamboo. **Typically 400–600 sq ft, single storey, 2 rooms and a verandah.**
Round bhunga type ≈ 18 ft diameter. Found extensively in Uttar Pradesh.
Build them with: uneven wall surfaces, a swept earth yard, a low mud boundary, a fodder stack,
a tethered animal, and a tiled or thatched roof that sags.

## 6 · THE TEMPLE
North Indian **shikhara** — the tower over the sanctum. Two families: **latina** (curved, over
the sanctum) and **phamsana** (straight-sided, bell-topped, over the hall).
Real small-village figures: a vimana 3.0 m square standing **3.68 m** total (base 1.68 +
body 1.20 + crown 0.80); another **4.80 m** (1.75 + 1.55 + 1.50). A 10'9" square shrine stands
**23 ft** with vertical walls to 17 ft and a pyramidal shikhara above.
So a modest hill or roadside temple: **3.5–5 m** tall for a shrine, **6–7 m** with a hall,
on a raised plinth with steps, whitewashed, saffron flag.
A **roadside shrine** is smaller still: ~1.4 m, whitewashed, red cloth, marigolds, a brass bell,
usually under a peepal.

## 7 · WHAT EVERY WALL GETS
A dust band on the **lower 0.6 m**. Water staining below the tank. At least one painted
advertisement or poster. **Exposed rebar** at the roof corners on a fraction of them — the
storey that never got built. Nothing is clean and nothing is plumb.

## 8 · STREET VENDOR CARTS AND STALLS  (added 3 Sep)
Manufacturer sizes, which is what actually exists on the street:
- **Tea cart (chai thela): ~4 ft long.**
- **Steel thela: 6 × 3 × 7 ft** (1.83 × 0.91 × 2.13 m) — the 7 ft includes the canopy frame.
- **Fruit/vegetable cart: 5 × 3 ft** (1.52 × 0.91 m), bed height ~0.75 m.
- Food vending cart bays **26 × 40 in**; other common sizes 23 × 59 in, 33 × 59 in, 36 × 68.5 in.
**Rule of thumb: 1.2–1.8 m long, 0.7–0.9 m wide, bed at 0.75 m, canopy at 2.0–2.2 m.**
Wheels are bicycle-type, ~0.6 m diameter, two at one end with a prop stand at the other.
A tarpaulin or sheet stretched on a light frame, weighted with bricks, sagging between corners.
