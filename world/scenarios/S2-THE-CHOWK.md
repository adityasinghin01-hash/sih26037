# S2 · THE CHOWK — the unsignalled gyratory
**Location: the real map, centre (+340, −580), radius 165 m.** Inherits [S0].

## WHAT IS ACTUALLY THERE — and it is better than a crossroads
919 m of road in 8 pieces, and the structure is unmistakable:
- **FOUR one-way tertiary roads**: 149 m at 46°, 145 m at 62°, 153 m at 232°, 157 m at 237°.
  Two going out, two coming back, in opposing pairs. **That is traffic circulating.**
- **NH534 trunk, 2 lanes, on BRIDGES** — 124 m at 239°, 121 m at 229°, plus 55 m at grade.
- **A junction at exactly (340.1, −579.9) where FIVE roads meet** — the highest-degree node
  in the whole 2 km box.
**So this is not two roads crossing. It is a gyratory: an island with one-way traffic circling
it, and the national highway passing over on bridges.** No signal, no lane markings on the
circulating carriageway. That is the real thing and it is far harder for a planner than a
signalled crossroads.

## THE GEOMETRY WE BUILD (IRC 65:2017 — the pairings are fixed, not free)
**Inscribed circle diameter 40 m → central island 24 m → circulatory carriageway 8 m.**
That is Table 6.2 and the three numbers must go together.
Rural/urban single lane, **design speed 30 km/h**. Turning radii for a 24 m island: entry
R1 ≈ 12 m, exit R2 ≈ 18 m. **Corner radii on the approach arms 12–15 m** (IRC SP-41).
Weaving length minimum 30 m at 30 km/h.
**Splitter islands** on each of the four arms, kerbed, 150 mm high, tapering.
**A truck apron is NOT provided** — the island is 24 m, large enough without one.
Give-way lines at each entry: **double line, 200 mm wide, 300 mm apart.**
Kerbs painted in black-and-white 500 mm bands, chipped, repainted over old paint.
**The circulating carriageway has no lane markings at all.** Vehicles take whatever line they
want. This is the fact the scenario turns on.

## THE MONUMENT — the centre of the island
A **person-scale statue on a plinth**, which is what a real small-town chowk has. Published data
is all about the giant 38 m monuments and is useless, so **this one is measured off Aditya's
footage against a known object, not guessed** — see REF-01 §14.
Working figure until measured: figure ~2.4 m on a **stepped plinth 2.6 m** high and 3.2 m
square, on a raised planted circle. Painted, garlanded, a small railing round it, four floodlights
on short posts aimed up (unlit at 06:45), pigeons on the head and shoulders, dropping stains.
Around it inside the island: unmown grass, two shrubs, a hoarding frame, litter blown against
the kerb, and a municipal signboard with the town name.

## THE FOUR ARMS — each one different, which is the point
**ARM A, 46° (north-east), the bazaar street.** Two-storey and three-storey shop-houses on both
sides, continuous frontage, no setback. The busiest arm.
**ARM B, 62° (east), to the highway.** Wider, fewer shops, a fuel pump 90 m out, hoardings.
**ARM C, 232° (south-west), residential.** Lower buildings, a school compound wall with a
painted mural, a temple 6.5 m with a shikhara and a saffron flag.
**ARM D, 237° (west), the old road.** Narrowest, worst surface, a tree in the middle of the
verge that the road bends around, a bus stop (5.5 × 1.6 × 2.8 m).
**Above it all: NH534 on its bridges**, deck about 6 m over the gyratory, piers 1800 × 1800 mm
landing in the verges, and the underside in permanent shade with pigeons and stains.

## PEOPLE — 132, and the crowd is LOCAL not spread
This is the busiest place in the world we are building, and the rule that makes it read as alive
is that **a real Indian street is crowded and empty at the same time** (Fruin).
18 at a **vegetable and fruit cluster**, arm A, 40–52 m: 5 squatting sellers behind spread
   tarpaulins, 9 buying, 4 passing through. In 38 m² → **0.47/m², "crowded".**
11 at a **chai stall**, arm A: 4 on a bench, 3 standing with glasses, 1 pouring, 3 waiting.
   In 24 m² → 0.46/m². Steam. A radio.
14 waiting for shared autos on arm B, a loose blob not a queue. 30 m² → 0.47/m².
9 at a sweet shop counter, arm A.
28 along the frontages in **7 separate groups of 3–5**, never a line.
19 walking, both directions, spaced 1.5–6 m, several in pairs.
8 children — 3 running, 1 on an oversized cycle, 4 squatting over something.
6 working: 2 raising shutters, 1 sweeping, 1 mending a cycle, 1 carrying a gas cylinder,
   1 at the hand pump.
7 crossing the circulating carriageway on foot, **in the traffic**, which is the normal way.
12 distant, 40–90 m, silhouettes in haze.
**And long empty stretches:** arm C has 4 people over 120 m — 0.02/m². Uniform spread is the tell.
Clothing: 40 % shirt-and-trousers, 25 % kurta, 20 % saree, 10 % salwar kameez, 5 % uniform.
**11 wear something saturated** — red, magenta, yellow. Those are the ones the eye finds and
they are placed deliberately, not randomly.

## THE ACTION — 48 seconds
t=0     Ego approaches on **arm D at 26 km/h**. Buildings tight, people at the edge.
t=5.2   Give-way line. **Nobody yields.** A motorcycle, then an auto, then a bus, all circulating.
        **BASELINE: stops at the line and stays there.** It needs a reference path made of
        waypoints and an unsignalled gyratory supplies none. **It cannot even start.**
t=6.0   **OURS: probe.** Noses forward 1.1 m at 0.4 m/s, watching whether the oncoming auto lifts.
t=8.3   The auto's speed drops 1.8 km/h. **Read as a yield.** Commit into the gap.
t=10.1  On the circulating carriageway. No lane markings — it picks its own line, 1.4 m off
        the island kerb.
t=14.6  **A motorcycle comes the wrong way round the island**, head on, at 18 km/h.
        Ego holds its line and lets it pass on the outside — **backing off now is more dangerous
        than continuing**, and the barrier underneath allows it.
t=19.0  Two cows lying on the island. A pedestrian steps off the kerb into the stream at 21.4 s.
t=24.8  Exit onto **arm A**, the bazaar. Speed 18. Crowd at 0.47 per square metre either side.
t=31.0  A cycle-rickshaw pulls out from the kerb without looking.
t=37.5  A parked Tata Ace narrows the lane to 2.9 m with people walking past it.
t=48.0  End.

## WHAT THIS TESTS — the strongest result we have
**Negotiation with agents who DO react**, unlike the cow. Probe, read the response, commit.
And the baseline failure here is **structural, not tuned**: MathWorks' shipped planner needs
Cartesian waypoints for a reference path. An unsignalled gyratory has none to give it. It is
not that we tuned it badly — it cannot start. That distinction is the whole result.
