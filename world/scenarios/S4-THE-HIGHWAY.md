# S4 · THE HIGHWAY — the interchange, the merge, and where we LOSE
**Location: the real map, centre (+130, −800), radius 235 m.** Inherits [S0].

## WHAT IS ACTUALLY THERE — the densest place in the box
**2848 m of road in 16 pieces** — three times any other zone. This is a real interchange where
two national highways meet and cross a road and a rail line on stacked bridges.
- **NH734, 2 lanes, one-way, on a BRIDGE** — 259 m at 359°
- **NH534, 2 lanes, on a BRIDGE** — 163 m at 249°
- another **trunk on a bridge**, 351 m at 175°, one-way
- a **trunk_link (slip road), 177 m at 235°, one-way** — the merge
- **two one-way tertiary roads** (340 m at 257°, 243 m at 73°) — the service roads beneath
- a secondary, 166 m at 56°, and 555 m + 251 m of residential behind
- **two junctions**, at (86, −694) and (101, −691), 15 m apart — a slip road splitting
**Nine of the box's eleven tagged bridges are here, and none of them crosses water.** They are
flyover decks. Building them as river bridges would have been a serious error.

## THE GEOMETRY WE BUILD
**Main carriageways: 2 × 3.5 m = 7.0 m each side, median 5.0 m raised** (IRC:86 desirable
minimum), median kerb **150 mm — the code maximum** — painted in black-and-white 500 mm bands.
Paved shoulder 1.5 m, earthen shoulder 1.0 m beyond.
**Markings (IRC 35, rural, ≤100 km/h): lane line 100 mm, 3 m mark + 6 m gap. Edge lines 150 mm
on both the shoulder side and the median side.**
**Flyover decks**: spans **22 m**, piers **1800 × 1800 mm** square, deck 6 m above the road
beneath, minimum vertical clearance **5.5 m**. Crash barriers along both deck edges.
Underneath: permanent shade, pigeon droppings, water staining, hoarding frames on the piers,
parked trucks, and a tea stall using the shelter.
**The slip road merge**: taper **1:40 desirable at 50–65 km/h → a 3.5 m lane needs 140 m of
taper** (IRC 35 Table 7.2). Ladder hatching in the closing lane, diagonals at 4 m, 100 mm wide.
**The lane drop after it**: four lanes to three at chainage 300, three to two at 440, each with
its own 140 m taper and hatching.
**Median opening for U-turns: 20 m, no storage lane** (NHAI practice). Ours sits in a 5 m median
— **the code says 9–12 m is needed merely to protect a crossing vehicle, and "even greater
widths are required for U-turns." So this U-turn is substandard by the code's own words.**
That is not a build error. It is the documented reality of Indian median cuts and it is exactly
why it is a hard planning case.

## THE ACTION — 71 seconds
t=0     Ego on the main carriageway at **58 km/h**. Structured traffic, lane markings, order.
t=4.0   **The planner detects structure and switches to lane-following mode.**
        This is the point of the scenario. See below.
t=9.5   Passes under the flyover. Sudden darkness, then light.
t=16.2  **Slip road merge.** Two vehicles come down the 177 m link and cross the 140 m taper.
        One accelerates hard; one hesitates and merges at 38 km/h into 58 km/h traffic.
t=24.0  **A truck begins a U-turn through the 20 m median opening** and cannot complete it in
        one movement. It stops across the fast lane and starts to reverse.
t=27.5  Ego changes lane left, checks the merging traffic is clear, passes behind the truck.
t=34.8  **Lane drop, four to three.** Hatching, then the lane closes. A motorcycle stays in the
        closing lane until the last 20 m.
t=42.0  **Three to two.** A second taper. The overloaded truck at 35 km/h is now the head of a
        queue and the ego must decide whether to overtake before the two-lane section.
t=51.0  A pedestrian crosses at grade in front of the queue.
t=58.0  Cattle on the median. One steps onto the shoulder.
t=71.0  End.

## WHAT THIS TESTS — and this is the scenario where we say WE LOSE
**Here, ordinary lane-based planners genuinely beat us.** This is the structured case that
methods like B-GAP were built for, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest and a judge
would find it.
**So the car detects that the road HAS structure and switches to lane-following.**
*"Our planner knows when it isn't needed."*
We put that on our own slide, before anyone asks. Nothing else buys that much credibility that
cheaply, and it converts a weakness into evidence of judgement.
What we DO claim here: the merge, the substandard U-turn and the double lane drop are handled,
and the mode switch itself is detected correctly and reported as a measured number.
