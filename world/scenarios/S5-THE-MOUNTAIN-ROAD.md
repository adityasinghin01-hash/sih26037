# S5 · THE MOUNTAIN ROAD — the river, the climb, and the edge that returns nothing
**Location: the real map, centre (−690, +760), radius 205 m, plus the hill we add.**
Inherits [S0].

## WHAT IS ACTUALLY THERE
493 m of road in seven pieces, and **the real river crossing of this whole box is here**:
- a **tertiary road with a BRIDGE, 89 m at 132°** — 46 m from the Malin's centreline
- a **residential road with a BRIDGE, 46 m at 162°** — 21 m from it
- 82 m and 43 m more of tertiary at 131° and 124°
- three residential pieces, and **a junction at (−765, +592) where three roads meet**
**Only two bridges in the entire 2 km box cross water, and both of them are here.**
Everything else tagged "bridge" is a flyover 1.5 km away in the south.

## WHAT WE ADD, AND WE SAY SO
**The hill.** Centred **(−1050, +900)** — **AMENDED 4 Sep 2026; the written (−690, +980) put the
hill on top of the Malin, five river centreline points inside its footprint. See S0 §3 for the
measurements and the three candidates.** Roughly 500 × 350 m at the base, **170 m** high, long
axis north-west. It now sits **386 m ahead on the approach** with **the river along its southern
toe** — so the ego crosses the Malin bridge and then climbs, which is what the ACTION below already
says. This is the one invented thing in the world and it is marked as ours on the
city plan. It is legitimate: Najibabad sits about 25 km from the real Shivaliks, and this belt
is where the plain meets the hills.
**Its shape is not free.** Shivalik catchments measure a **drainage density of 4.55 km of
channel per km²** and a stream frequency of 9.36 per km². So the flanks carry a **branching
network of many small gullies**, not one or two token creases. Ridged, with rock showing on the
upper third, a small quarry scar on the south-west face, and sal forest below.
**A seasonal waterfall** on the north flank, 22 m over a rock step, alive in late September and
reduced to a dark stain by winter. Visible from the road at about 400 m, small in frame, with
its own bright patch of vegetation around it.

## THE ROAD — and hill roads are not straight or well made
**Approach (0–180 m): the real tertiary road**, 7.0 m, then the **bridge over the Malin**:
carriageway **7.5 m** (IRC 5, two lanes), **footpath 1.5 m clear on the west side only**,
**safety kerb 750 mm on the east** — deliberately asymmetric, which is what the real one is.
**Railings 1.1 m** above the deck, bottom gap ≤150 mm, steel tube and vertical bar panels,
white and blue paint, rust bleeding at every weld, **three panels bent** where something hit them.
Deck heavily potholed and patch-repaired: **16 patches, 11 open potholes 0.2–0.7 m.**
Approach embankments **90 m each side** — a road cannot be lifted onto a deck in 30 m without
becoming a 9 % ramp, and the limit is 5 %.
**The macaques are on this bridge.** See ANIMALS.

**The climb (180–1150 m): single lane, and deteriorating.**
Carriageway **3.75 m** (IRC hill road), widening to **9.0 m at each hairpin apex** for
long-wheelbase vehicles. **Superelevation 1 in 10.**
**Four hairpins**, inner radius **14 m**, transition curves 15 m, **minimum 60 m between
successive bends** — all IRC 52. Design speed 20 km/h.
**The surface is not uniform, and that is the point:**
| stretch | surface |
|---|---|
| 180–340 m | good bitumen, recently laid, clean edges |
| 340–520 m | old bitumen, cracked, edges broken away by 200–400 mm |
| **520–640 m** | **no bitumen at all** — graded earth and loose stone, a washout being repaired |
| 640–860 m | patched, a long tar seam down the middle, potholes at the inside of bends |
| 860–1150 m | good again, near the temple |

**THE TWO SIDES ARE NOT THE SAME, AND EVERYTHING TURNS ON THIS.**
**Inside (hill side):** a cut face. Rock and soil, 3–12 m high, in places held by a **dry stone
breast wall** (banded masonry, ≤6 m, IS 14458), in places by **gabion baskets** (1 m modules,
wire crates of stone, the flexible option where the foundation is poor), and in one 60 m stretch
by **rockfall netting** pinned to the face. A **catch drain** at the toe, 400 mm, half full of
fallen stone. Loose scree on the road at 470 m and 605 m.
**Outside (the drop):** **a parapet only where somebody built one.**
| stretch | edge |
|---|---|
| 180–420 m | 0.6 m stone parapet, continuous, whitewashed with black bands |
| **420–505 m** | **PARAPET GONE.** 14 m of it knocked out and never replaced. Rubble below |
| 505–700 m | parapet present but broken at 6 places, leaning outward at 2 |
| **700–790 m** | **no parapet was ever built here.** Just the edge, and 40 m of air |
| 790–1150 m | new W-beam barrier, 700 mm, posts at 2.0 m — a recent upgrade |
**Drop below the unprotected sections: 25–60 m onto boulders.**

**Culverts:** five hume pipes, 900 mm, at 265, 398, 542, 706 and 941 m, with splayed wing walls,
all discharging over the edge and staining the rock face below. Two are blocked with silt and
the water has cut a channel across the road instead.
**Landslide works:** at **560–620 m** a slip has taken the outer half of the road. A **JCB 3DX**
(5.9 × 2.35 × 3.61 m, yellow, filthy, outriggers down) is working there, with a tipper, a heap
of aggregate, stacked hume pipes and **red-and-white plastic barriers** — exactly the kit in
drive_13_t107. **Single-lane working past it, controlled by nobody.**

## THE TEMPLE — at the top, 1150 m
A small hill temple: sanctum with a **latina shikhara**, whitewashed, **5.2 m** overall on a
raised plinth, a small pillared hall in front, saffron flags on bamboo, a bell hung at the
entrance, tiles underfoot worn smooth. **38 stone steps** up from a small levelled parking area
where the road ends in a loop. A peepal beside it with red thread wound round the trunk.
Two shops at the parking: prasad, flowers, cold drinks, tin-roofed, 3.0 m shutters.
A water tank, a hand pump, monkeys on the roofs.

## HILLSIDE SETTLEMENT — 22 buildings
Not a town. A scatter along the road where the slope allows.
7 single-storey stone-and-mud houses with **slate or tin roofs**, built into the cut, their
uphill wall half retaining wall. Terraced plots behind them, 3–8 m wide, dug into the slope.
5 tea shops and small dhabas at the wider points, always on the **inside** of a bend where
there is room — a charpai, a kettle, plastic chairs, a hoarding for a mobile network.
4 tin sheds. 3 cattle byres. 2 half-built houses with rebar. 1 forest department hut, painted.
**One mobile tower**, 30 m, on the shoulder at 880 m, with a shed and a diesel generator.
**11 kV poles follow the road all the way up**, but the spans are shorter (30 m) because the
ground is broken, and there are stays at every bend.

## VEGETATION
**Sal forest** below and around, instanced, densest at the foot, thinning with altitude, bare
rock on the upper third. **Undergrowth follows light**: 0.90 cover at the forest edge, 0.55 in
the two clearings, **0.15 under closed canopy** — mostly bare leaf litter there.
**Soil is two-tone and the two tones are placed by physics:** dark, near-black humus in the
hollows and gullies where litter collects and stays damp; **light pebbly tan on ridges and
sunlit slopes** where it washes off and dries — and Bhabar soils genuinely carry small stones
through the top layer. The boundary is a noise-masked gradient, never a line.
Leaf litter 60–120 mm deep in hollows, 0–10 mm on ridges. Sal leaves are large and leathery, so
they read individually rather than as a mat.
22 fallen limbs and 9 standing dead stems, **all lying downslope, not across it.**
Kans grass on the road edges and the river banks. Ferns in the wet gully mouths.

## ANIMALS
**THE MACAQUE TROOP — 24 rhesus, on and around the bridge.** This is the frame Aditya named.
Body 47–53 cm, tail 21–23 cm, height on all fours 41–57 cm.
Composition from field data (≈2 adult females per adult male): **5 adult males, 10 adult
females — 3 carrying infants on the belly — and 9 juveniles.**
6 on the parapet rail, 8 walking the footpath in a loose line, 4 on the deck itself, 3 grooming
on the kerb, 3 in the tree at the far abutment.
**Tails up and curved — that is the silhouette that identifies them at any distance.**
They scatter to the rail when the car is within ~6 m and come straight back.
More monkeys at the temple: 9, on the roofs and the steps, one on the bell.
4 goats on the cut face, standing where no goat should be able to.
2 dogs at the roadworks. Cattle at two of the byres.
Birds: fewer than the plain, and different — 2 kites riding the slope lift, crows at the temple,
a whistling thrush near the waterfall, no pigeons.

## THE ACTION — 88 seconds
t=0     Ego on the approach at **34 km/h**. Fields give way to the river.
t=6.0   **The bridge.** Narrow, potholed, railings both sides.
        **The macaque troop scatters to the rail at 7.8 s** and comes back behind us.
t=11.5  A motorcycle oncoming **in the middle of the deck**. Ego moves left; deck is 7.5 m.
t=16.0  Off the bridge, the climb begins. Speed 22. Surface good.
t=24.0  **First hairpin.** 14 m inner radius, apex widened to 9 m, 10 % superelevation.
t=31.5  Surface degrades. Cracked edges. **Parapet ends at 420 m — 14 m of nothing.**
        **The drop returns NO lidar points.** It is not detected; it is absent.
        The car must reason about **where it expected ground and got none.**
        **Margin is asymmetric: scraping the rock face is a dented panel, going over is fatal.**
        So it hugs the inside, 0.6 m off the cut, not the centre of the road.
t=38.0  **A bus comes down.** Road is 3.75 m; a bus is 2.6 m and we are 1.7 m. **No room.**
        **Indian law: on a road too narrow to pass, the vehicle going DOWNHILL gives precedence
        to the one coming UP.** Ego holds. The bus reverses 40 m to the hairpin apex where the
        road is 9 m wide. **A codified statutory give-way rule, and we follow it.**
t=47.0  Past the bus. **The washout: no bitumen, loose stone, single-lane working past the JCB.**
        Speed 8 km/h. The flagman waves us through.
t=56.0  Second and third hairpins. **Speed limited by what can be seen** — as sight distance
        shrinks, safe speed shrinks with it, by the same rule that governs sensor degradation.
t=64.0  **700–790 m: no parapet was ever built.** 40 m of air. The car offsets further inside.
t=71.0  The waterfall visible across the valley, small, bright.
t=79.0  Fourth hairpin. The mobile tower. The temple flags come into view.
t=88.0  The parking loop at the top. End.

## WHAT THIS TESTS — the danger that gives no signal
Every other scenario is about things that are *there*. This one is about **something that isn't**.
A cliff edge reflects nothing back to a laser. It is structurally invisible, so it can never be
"detected" — it has to be **authored as ground truth in the scenario and reasoned about as an
absence of expected return.** That is a different kind of safety argument from every other case,
and it is the honest reason the road-edge barrier exists separately from the obstacle barrier.
Plus: a statutory give-way rule, single-lane working, and a surface that is genuinely bad in
places and fine in others — because real hill roads are exactly that.
